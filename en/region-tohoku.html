Northern Honshu

Tohoku carries most of the injuries

Of the bear injuries recorded across Japan in 2025, more than 60% were in the Tohoku region. Akita Prefecture alone accounted for 67.

Gear and behaviour do not guarantee that you will avoid an encounter or injury. Check the municipality and the trail or park manager for the mountain you choose.

Last updated: September 23, 2026

The 2025 figures

Nationally, 238 people were injured and 13 died. That is higher than the previous peak in 2023, when the figures were 219 injured and 6 deaths. Tohoku accounted for over 60% of the national total, with Akita at 67.

About 70% of injuries occurred inside forest, and about 20% happened while people were gathering mushrooms or wild plants. That second figure matters for visitors: a large share of incidents involve people who left the trail to look at the ground, not people walking a route.

The Shimokita Peninsula is a separate case

The population on the Shimokita Peninsula, at the northern tip of Honshu, is listed by the Ministry of the Environment as a local population at risk of extinction. As with western Japan, a small population is a conservation figure and not a measure of how likely you are to meet one. Prepare the same way you would anywhere else on Honshu.

What this page cannot tell you

What is set out above is national statistics and the population picture. We have not confirmed how individual mountain areas in Tohoku are managed — which trails have notices, what local rules apply at huts and campsites, or how sightings are published. Those vary by prefecture and by site.

For preparation and encounter behaviour, see black bear guidance, which follows Nagano Prefecture's advice for the Asian black bear. Then check the municipality and the trail or park manager for your specific destination. To start again from the region level, go back to Choose a hiking region.
