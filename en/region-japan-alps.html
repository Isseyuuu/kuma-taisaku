Northern Alps · Kamikochi

Planning to hike the Japanese Alps?

Asian black bears live throughout the Northern Alps, including the valley from Kamikochi up to Yokoo. This page covers only what the Ministry of the Environment has published for the Kamikochi area.

Carrying gear and following guidance do not guarantee that you will avoid an encounter or injury. Check current notices from the municipality and the site manager for your route before you go.

Last updated: September 23, 2026

The species here is the Asian black bear

The estimated population of the northern Northern-Alps regional group has a median of 600 bears. Kamikochi records roughly 40 to 60 sightings a year; in a high year there were 158 between mid-April and mid-November. These are area-wide historical counts, not an indication of conditions on the trail today.

What hikers are asked to carry

Carry a bear bell or something else that makes noise. Shake it before you set out and confirm it actually sounds. Early morning, dusk and bad weather call for extra care.

Sightings concentrate around the Dakesawa and Tashiro wetlands, where boardwalks run through marshy ground with poor visibility. If your walk includes them, read the on-site notices first.

Shiretoko asks for something different

For Kamikochi, the published guidance is about carrying a noise-maker. In Shiretoko, bear spray is treated as basic equipment for hikers, and food must be stored in food lockers at designated campsites. Prepare for the species and the local rules at your destination, not for "Japan" in general.

If you see a bear

Do not approach, do not photograph, and do not startle it. Back away slowly without turning your back. Never photograph a cub; the mother is nearby. In 2009 nine people were injured on Mount Norikura.

General guidance for Asian black bears, based on Nagano Prefecture's published advice, is on our encounter guide.

Who to contact, and what to check

To report a sighting in Kamikochi, contact the Kamikochi Information Center at 0263-95-2433. The Ministry of the Environment also publishes a Kamikochi bear countermeasures manual (Japanese). We link to it without summarising it, because we have not been able to read its contents.

Do not judge the whole Northern Alps from Kamikochi information alone. Check the municipality and site manager covering the section you will walk. To compare regions again, return to choosing a hiking region.
