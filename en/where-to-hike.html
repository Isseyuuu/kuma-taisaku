Start here · Japan hiking

Are there bears where you plan to hike?

Start with the island. Hokkaido has brown bears; parts of Honshu and Shikoku have Asian black bears. Kyushu's wild black-bear population is considered extinct. This is a regional filter, not a live alert or a judgment about any particular trail.

Distribution sources: WWF Japan and the Ministry of the Environment. Last reviewed: September 23, 2026.

Step 1 · Choose an island

Bear distribution by region

These island-level descriptions cannot tell you whether a bear is present on a particular route on a particular day. Choose your region, then use the next-step checklist below.

01

Hokkaido

Brown bear · ヒグマ

Found across much of Hokkaido. WWF describes an adult head-and-body length of about 200–230 cm and weight of about 150–250 kg. This is a different species from the black bear on Honshu and Shikoku.

Next: look for current notices from the municipality and trail or park manager for your destination.
Hokkaido and Shiretoko →

02

Honshu

Asian black bear · ツキノワグマ

Present in parts of Honshu, not uniformly across the island. WWF gives a typical head-and-body length of about 110–130 cm; location-specific presence still needs checking.

Next: check the municipality and trail or park manager for the exact mountain you have chosen.
By area: Japanese Alps · Tokyo and Okutama · Tohoku · Western Japan

03

Shikoku

Asian black bear · ツキノワグマ

A small, threatened population remains. Do not treat the whole island as bear-free or use population size to infer the situation on one trail.

Next: check current local information for your destination before hiking.
By area: Western Japan

04

Kyushu

No established wild bear population

Kyushu's Asian black-bear population is considered regionally extinct. This is a distribution statement, not a guarantee about every wildlife report or every hiking condition.

Next: still check current trail conditions and local notices for the mountain you choose.
By area: Kyushu

Step 2 · Check your destination

Before committing to a trail

  1. Identify the island and exact mountain or trail. The cards above answer only the first part.
  2. Find the municipality or site manager's latest information. Check the date and the area covered by any notice.
  3. Use species-specific official guidance. Our encounter guide cites Nagano Prefecture's recommendations for Asian black bears. It must not be applied to Hokkaido brown bears without separate verification.

Sources and limits

This page describes broad distribution and indicative adult size, not a risk ranking. Size figures are from a WWF Japan explainer published in 2012; they cannot tell you the current situation on your trail. Use current local notices for that decision.
