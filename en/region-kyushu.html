Outside the range

Kyushu is outside the bear range

The wild Asian black bear population of Kyushu is considered extinct. The last confirmed record was in 1957, and the Ministry of the Environment judged the population extinct in 2012.

Last updated: September 23, 2026

What this does, and does not, tell you

This is a statement about distribution. It is not a statement that a Kyushu mountain is fine in terms of wildlife generally, weather, or trail conditions. Wild boar, insects, sudden weather and route closures are checked the same way in Kyushu as anywhere else: through the municipality and the trail or park manager for the mountain you pick.

If Kyushu is the only part of Japan you plan to hike, the bear preparation described elsewhere on this site does not apply to your trip.

If your trip includes other regions

Many itineraries pair Kyushu with Honshu or Hokkaido. The preparation is not interchangeable. Honshu and Shikoku have the Asian black bear; Hokkaido has the brown bear, which is a different species and a much larger animal. Work through Choose a hiking region for each place you will actually walk, rather than carrying one set of assumptions across the whole trip.
