Brown bear country

Before you hike in Hokkaido or Shiretoko

Brown bears are found across most of Hokkaido, and Shiretoko is one of the densest brown bear habitats in the world. Do not bring a Honshu black-bear plan to Shiretoko unchanged.

Brown bears occupy about 55% of Hokkaido's land area, with an estimated median of 11,700 animals as of FY2020. That is an area-wide estimate; conditions on your route have to come from current local information.

Last updated: September 23, 2026

Bear spray is treated as basic equipment

For hiking in Shiretoko, bear spray is described as basic equipment. We do not make claims about how well it works and we do not give instructions for using it. Read the Ministry of the Environment and site manager guidance before you travel.

How this differs from Kamikochi

In Kamikochi, in the Northern Alps, the published advice is to carry a bear bell or another noise-maker. Shiretoko adds bear spray as basic equipment and specific rules for storing food overnight.

Food goes in the lockers at designated campsites

The designated campsites on the Shiretoko range — Rausu-daira, Mitsumine, Futatsu-ike and Daiichi-kako — have food lockers. Seal food in plastic and put it in the locker.

Elsewhere, use a portable bear-resistant food container, or hang your food from a tree.

While you are walking

Make noise where visibility is poor, and carry a bear bell. Stay away from animal carcasses, which draw bears in.

For encounters, use Hokkaido guidance

The encounter advice on our black bear guidance page comes from Nagano Prefecture and describes Asian black bears on Honshu. Do not transfer it to brown bears without checking. Use guidance from Hokkaido authorities and the manager of the area you are entering.

Before departure, check current information from whoever manages the trail and campsite you plan to use. To compare regions again, return to choosing a hiking region.
