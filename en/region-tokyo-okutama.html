Tama area, including Mount Takao

There are bears in Tokyo

Asian black bears live in the forests of Tokyo's Tama area. This is not only remote Okutama — Hachioji, which contains Mount Takao, is on the list too.

Gear and behaviour do not guarantee that you will avoid an encounter or injury. Alongside the city-wide information below, check the municipality and site manager for the trail you choose.

Last updated: September 23, 2026

Where they are recorded

The municipalities involved are Okutama, Hinohara, Akiruno, Ome, Hachioji and Hinode. Mount Takao is in Hachioji.

A 2025 survey estimated 120 to 378 bears, averaging 235, at a density of 0.47 per km², and the trend is increasing. At the same time the species is listed as VU (endangered) in southern Tama and NT (near threatened) in western Tama, and hunting is prohibited until March 2027.

Make noise as you walk

Carry a bear bell and make noise as you walk. Shake the bell before you set out and confirm it sounds. Tokyo's guidance describes bears as generally timid and inclined to avoid people, but says they attack when startled or cornered.

Give a mother with cubs the widest berth. Tokyo describes that as the most dangerous situation. For general encounter behaviour, see our black bear guidance, which is based on Nagano Prefecture's advice.

Store food away from camp

If you camp, keep food away from your campsite. Ask the site manager where and how food should be stored at the place you are using.

Check each destination, Takao included

A city-wide estimate says nothing about conditions on the day. Before you leave, check notices from your destination's municipality — Okutama, Hachioji and so on — and from the trail manager.
